Credit Score Myths That Could Be Hurting Your Finances (2025)

Your credit score can impact everything from getting approved for an apartment to qualifying for a car loan. But despite its importance, many people still believe outdated or completely false information about how credit scores work.

In 2025, misinformation spreads fast online. This article breaks down common credit score myths that could be affecting your finances - and what the truth really is.

Why Credit Score Myths Matter

A good credit score can open doors. A bad one can quietly cost you thousands over time through higher interest rates, rejections, or higher deposits.

Believing the wrong things about your score can lead to missed opportunities or unnecessary financial stress.

Let’s clear up the confusion.

Myth 1: Checking Your Own Credit Hurts Your Score

Reality: This is one of the most common myths - and it’s completely false.

When you check your own credit (also known as a “soft inquiry”), it has no impact on your credit score. Only “hard inquiries” - like those from lenders during an application - may slightly lower your score temporarily.

In fact, regularly checking your credit is a smart habit. You can spot errors, track improvements, and stay informed.

Myth 2: Closing Credit Cards Improves Your Score

Reality: Closing a credit card may actually hurt your score, not help it.

That’s because closing a card reduces your total available credit. This can increase your credit utilization ratio - a key factor in your score.

If the card has no annual fee and a long history, it may be better to keep it open and use it occasionally.

Myth 3: You Need to Carry a Balance to Build Credit

Reality: You do not need to carry debt or pay interest to improve your credit.

Simply using your card for small purchases and paying it off in full each month shows responsible usage. Carrying a balance just costs you more in interest.

Myth 4: Your Income Affects Your Credit Score

Reality: Your income is not part of your credit score calculation.

While lenders might look at income when deciding whether to approve you, your credit score is based on your credit activity - things like payment history, balances, and age of accounts.

That means someone with a lower income can still have an excellent credit score if they manage their credit well.

Myth 5: Credit Reports Are Always Accurate

Reality: Credit reports can and do contain mistakes.

That’s why it’s important to check your report at least once a year. In the U.S., you’re entitled to free credit reports from the major bureaus through official portals.

Spot an error? You can file a dispute - often online - to have it corrected.

Understanding these myths can help you protect your credit without unnecessary stress.

FAQ: Credit Score Basics

Q: What is considered a “good” credit score?
A: Scores generally range from 300 to 850. A score of 670 and above is often considered good, though definitions vary by lender.

Q: How often should I check my credit?
A: At least once a year, but more often if you’re actively working on credit or preparing for a loan.

Q: Can I improve my score quickly?
A: There are no instant fixes. However, paying on time, reducing balances, and avoiding new credit applications can steadily raise your score.

Q: What’s the biggest factor in a credit score?
A: Payment history is usually the most important, followed by credit utilization and age of credit.

Final Thoughts

Credit scores might seem mysterious, but they follow predictable patterns. By understanding and avoiding common myths, you can make smarter decisions that benefit you over time.

Small habits - like paying on time and checking your report - can make a big difference.
